Where’s the Folder?

November 24, 2026 · Ken Robertson

All posts

I’ve sat with a lot of families after a loss.

Real estate tends to stay in a family for decades, so as a property advisor I’m often in the room when it changes hands. The conversations tend to follow the same pattern.

Usually there was a plan. There’s a trust, and an attorney who drew it up years ago. And still, the person across the table is asking the same thing: What do I actually need to do now? Can I do it myself?

Something I’m seeing: most families don’t lack a plan. What they lack is a clear next step, and someone they can ask without running the meter.

Here’s a summary of the list I walk through:

  • Know what you own, how it’s titled, and that it’s properly listed in the trust.
  • Get a value as of the date of death. It usually sets the new tax basis.
  • Check your insurance. Coverage often needs updating after a death.
  • Gather the leases. Know who pays what.
  • Know your team: attorney, CPA, advisor.

None of that is complicated. What stays with me is something people say in almost every one of these meetings, in one form or another: the property is still there, but it doesn’t feel the same without them.

No checklist covers that part. Real estate is always about people first.

The best meetings are the ones where a son, a daughter or a grandchild is sitting at the table too, taking notes and asking for their own copy. The next generation is in the room before they have to be.

This week, a lot of families will be at one table. Nobody needs to talk about estates over pie. But if there’s property in your family, it’s a good week to make sure someone besides Mom and Dad knows where the folder is.

No agenda here. If the full list would be useful, just email me and I’ll send it.

Remembering Spencer
